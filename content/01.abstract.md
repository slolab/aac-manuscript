## Abstract

Agentic AI prototypes are being deployed across domains with increasing speed, yet tools for their prospective planning are lagging behind.
Existing AI documentation practices and guidelines – Model Cards, Datasheets, or NIST AI RMF – are either retrospective or lack machine-readability and interoperability.
We present a structured approach to the prospective design of agentic systems, including a FAIR specification and a canvas to facilitate its collaborative use by users and developers: the Agentic Automation Canvas (AAC).
The AAC specification captures six dimensions of an automation project: definition and scope; user expectations with quantified benefit metrics; developer feasibility assessments; governance staging; data access and sensitivity; and outcomes.
It is implemented as a semantic web-compatible metadata schema with controlled vocabulary and mappings to established ontologies such as Schema.org and W3C DCAT, and is made accessible through a privacy-preserving, fully client-side web application with real-time validation.
Completed canvases export as RO-Crates with FAIR-aligned metadata, yielding versioned, shareable, and machine-interoperable project contracts between users and developers.
We held 28 consultations on diverse applications in health, life science, and technology, with users from academia and industry.
We found that the usefulness of the canvas derives from operational specifics, such as usage patterns, maintenance ownership, and data access restrictions; however, this level of detail made it difficult for users to fill out the canvas on their own.
We therefore created a two-stage framework – a simplified entry canvas and the full specification – to guide users and developers through the planning process.
The AAC and its web application are available as open-source code and interactive web form at [https://aac.slolab.ai](https://aac.slolab.ai).
