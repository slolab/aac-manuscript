## Discussion {.page_break_before}

The formative observations from completed canvases and exploratory design engagements qualify how the AAC's benefit estimates and design decisions should be interpreted.
Benefit estimates should be recorded but remain open to revision.
The value of the canvas is not that it ensures accurate predictions, but that it records the prediction, its assumptions, and the planned evaluation so that expected and realised outcomes can later be compared.
Selected completed AAC use cases are being followed prospectively, with expectations recorded before implementation to enable this comparison.
Detailed qualitative observations supporting these points are provided in Supplementary Note 1.

Agentic designs should be compared with appropriate alternatives.
The relevant question is what capability the agent provides beyond the strongest non-agentic alternative.
Reuse may prevent unnecessary duplication, but similar workflows may also be justified by local data, licensing, infrastructure, privacy, or scientific requirements.
The AAC does not require reuse; instead, comparable project descriptions make the choice between reuse and independent development more visible.

However, outside of projects with our direct involvement, a significant limitation of the decentralised private-by-design RO-Crates as the only output of the canvas is the need for additional mechanisms for sharing and finding them.
In the future, we aim to integrate the resulting metadata repository more closely with agentic tooling such as our MCP server registry [@doi:10.1038/s41587-025-02900-9], the registry for our knowledge graph framework components [@doi:10.1038/s41587-023-01848-y], and community research software platforms [@doi:10.1186/s13059-019-1772-6], in order to allow the broad reuse and recomposition of open-source agentic workflows.

Taken together, these observations suggest that the main value of the AAC lies less in predicting whether a project will succeed than in structuring the design conversation and helping stakeholders ask better, more systematic questions of one another.
Planning should extend beyond technical implementation to maintenance and adoption.
The AAC is therefore best understood as a living design and communication artifact rather than a fixed checklist or an enforceable contract.
We welcome contributors and use cases from the community, which we will continually support through open-source mechanisms.
