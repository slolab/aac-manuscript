## Discussion {.page_break_before}

Benefit estimates should be treated as revisable assumptions rather than reliable forecasts. Their value lies in recording expectations, assumptions, and evaluation plans so that expected and realised outcomes can later be compared. Agentic designs should also be assessed against the strongest practical non-agentic alternative. Supporting and qualifying observations are presented in Supplementary Note 1, "Testing Expectations and Alternatives".

Reuse may prevent unnecessary duplication, but local development may be justified by data, licensing, infrastructure, privacy, or scientific requirements. The AAC makes this choice and its rationale more explicit. A related limitation is that private-by-design RO-Crates require additional mechanisms to make shared canvases findable and reusable. Future integration with our MCP server registry [@doi:10.1038/s41587-025-02900-9], knowledge graph component registry [@doi:10.1038/s41587-023-01848-y], and community research software platforms [@doi:10.1186/s13059-019-1772-6] could support the discovery and recomposition of open-source agentic workflows. Further observations are provided in Supplementary Note 1, "Discoverability, Reuse, and Duplicated Development".

The main value of the AAC lies less in predicting project success than in structuring the conversation between users and developers. It provides a living record of assumptions, constraints, responsibilities, and evaluation criteria, while extending planning beyond implementation to deployment, maintenance, ownership, and adoption. The AAC is therefore best understood as a living design and communication artifact rather than a fixed checklist or an enforceable contract. Related evidence is discussed in Supplementary Note 1, "Maintenance, Deployment, and Adoption".
