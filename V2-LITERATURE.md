## What changes the manuscript

1. **Soften the abstract's opening claim.** "No methodology for their structured design, governance, and prospective evaluation has been established" is no longer literally defensible. Singapore's IMDA published a governance framework specific to agentic AI (v1.0 January 2026, updated 2026-05-20), and Microsoft shipped an "agent design framework" with a downloadable design canvas (2026-02-23, one week after our preprint). Neither offers a per-project design artifact with quantified benefit contracts and machine-readable FAIR export, so the specific contribution stands. Reword to something like "no per-project design and documentation methodology with machine-readable output", and cite the nearest neighbors.
2. **Position against Kasirzadeh and Gabriel, "Agentic profiles for effective AI governance", Nature, August 2026** (@doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10805-z, verified). Proposes characterizing agents along autonomy, efficacy, goal complexity, and generality for deployment and oversight decisions. This is the closest published relative of the AAC and a reviewer will expect it. Differentiation: agentic profiles classify a system for governance; the AAC structures the negotiation and record before and during development.
3. **The METR citation needs its follow-up.** METR revised its developer-productivity experiment on 2026-02-24 (verified: interim estimates -18% for returning and -4% for new developers, both confidence intervals now include speedups; 30-50% of developers withheld tasks they expected AI to be fast on; METR calls its estimates likely lower bounds and abandons the design). If @arxiv:2507.09089 stays, cite the update alongside it: @url:https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/.
4. **Split the facilitation gap into two components before writing the discussion lead.** The interviewing-skill component is being automated and has citable counter-evidence (OntoAgent, AInterviewer below). The anchoring component (opening the tool pre-commits the user to agents) is untouched by that work and has fresh supporting evidence (Lin et al. anchoring experiment, Parisi et al. LLM facilitation, CHI Funding AI for Good). Resting the claim on the anchoring component makes it robust to review.
5. **Beat 8.4 phrasing: "planning and context data", not "training data".** No in-window work trains LLMs on design artifacts. The in-window evidence is for design documents as agent context and planning input (ADR context study, CodeSpec, SpecBench below), and it is strong.
6. **Methods accuracy at submission time.** RO-Crate 1.3 was released 2026-06-23 (trivial migration from 1.2: update conformsTo and @context). The official MCP registry is still in preview as of August 2026; say "preview". The MCP specification had a major revision on 2026-07-28 (stateless protocol, discovery RPC).
7. **Intro citation upgrade.** "Agentic AI is rapidly emerging across all fields of science" is now supportable with wet-lab-validated flagship papers instead of reviews: Google Co-Scientist and FutureHouse Robin (Nature, May 2026) and Biomni (Science, July 2026).
8. **Croissant 1.1 (2026-02-12) strengthens beat 8.2 beyond precedent.** It added PROV-O provenance and DUO/ODRL governance terms, the same vocabularies the AAC uses, and explicitly targets agent-readable datasets. 700,000+ datasets carry Croissant metadata.
9. **Positioning table candidates:** Agent Cards (MICAI proceedings, Feb 2026, operational documentation of deployed agents), A2A Agent Card v1.0 (March 2026, interoperability metadata), Open Agent Specification (ACM CAIS 2026, implementation portability). All are design-time or runtime descriptions of built agents; none covers prospective project design. Adding one or two rows would sharpen the table.
10. **Name collisions.** "Agent canvas" is now overloaded: Microsoft's agent design canvas, OpenHands Agent Canvas (June 2026, a UI workspace), Cisco AI Canvas (June 2026, an ops workspace), and, out of window but predating v1, Translucent Computing's "Agentic AI Canvas" (March 2025, a consultancy business canvas, near-identical name). Consider a disambiguating sentence; acknowledging Translucent preempts a reviewer finding it.

## Abstract and opening claims: competing and neighboring frameworks

- **IMDA Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI** (Singapore, v1.0 2026-01-22, updated 2026-05-20). Four pillars covering design through deployment; described as the first agentic-AI-specific governance framework. Organizational risk guidance, no per-project artifact, no machine-readable export. Cite and reword claim (a). @url:https://www.imda.gov.sg/resources/press-releases-factsheets-and-speeches/updated-model-ai-governance-framework-for-agentic-ai
- **Microsoft agent design framework and canvas** (Microsoft Learn, 2026-02-23, updated 2026-05-21, verified). Ten building blocks: Goal, Triggers, Tools, Channels, Knowledge and data, Flows and orchestration, Instructions, Architecture, Governance and risk, Evaluation. Downloadable .docx canvas, framed as "a thinking aid, not rigid documentation". Strongest in-window near-duplicate; vendor-tied, no schema, no benefit quantification, no contract. @url:https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-studio/guidance/agent-design-canvas-framework
- **Agent Cards** (Urteaga-Reyesvera and Lopez Murphy, Springer LNAI, MICAI workshops proceedings, published 2026-02-19). Structured documentation of deployed agents: roles, memory, tools, monitoring, governance scope. The closest new entry in the Model Card lineage; documents operational agents, so the "retrospective" characterization extends to it. @doi:10.1007/978-3-032-17933-3_25
- **CAGE-1** (Sure, preprint, 2026-07-03). Pre-deployment readiness evaluation for enterprise agents across twelve dimensions. Competes with "prospective evaluation" narrowly; single-author preprint, assurance-oriented, no design or communication role. @arxiv:2607.03510
- **Open Agent Specification (Agent Spec)** (Oracle, peer-reviewed at ACM CAIS 2026, May 2026; technical report is pre-window). Framework-agnostic declarative language for defining agents portably across runtimes. Machine-readable and design-time, but specifies implementation, without benefits, feasibility, or governance staging. Also note: ACM launched CAIS (Conference on AI and Agentic Systems) itself in 2026.
- **A2A protocol v1.0** (Linux Foundation, 2026-03-12; v1.0.1 2026-05-28; 150+ organizations). The Agent Card is now a stable standard for machine-readable self-description of deployed agents. Update the positioning discussion to v1.0; still operational metadata, supports our framing.
- **2025 AI Agent Index** (Staufer et al., FAccT 2026; preprint 2026-02-19). Documents 30 deployed agentic systems; finds sparse, inconsistent, retrospective documentation of safety and evaluation. Direct empirical support for "conceived ad hoc, evaluated ad hoc". @arxiv:2602.17753
- **NIST CAISI AI Agent Standards Initiative** (launched 2026-02-17; ISO/IEC JTC 1 track, NSF-cofunded protocol work; SP 800-53 agent overlays still in draft). Supports "standards not yet established" for governance staging.
- **Governance Gaps in Agent Interoperability Protocols** (Kang and Diponegoro, preprint, 2026-06-30). Analyzes MCP, A2A, ACP across six governance dimensions; governance is "a missing architectural layer". Independent confirmation that protocol artifacts cannot express governance. @arxiv:2606.31498
- **EU AI Act draft guidelines on high-risk classification** (European Commission, 2026-05-19). Agentic systems assessed holistically; Article 50 transparency obligations apply from August 2026 and Commission guidance extends them to agents. Supports the governance discussion: regulation demands documented intended purpose before deployment.
- **CASE Framework** (Telukunta, Lilis, Baron, preprint, 2026-08-10). Layered control architecture for running enterprise agent fleets with a maturity model. Operational counterpart to the AAC's planning stage. @arxiv:2608.10153
- **Policy Cards status**: no journal version, no new arXiv version in the window. In-window citers (@arxiv:2604.05229 runtime guardrails, 2026-04-06; @arxiv:2603.20833, 2026-03-21) and the related "Policies on Paths" (@arxiv:2603.16586, 2026-03-17) all sit at the runtime layer, confirming our characterization and leaving the planning slot open.
- **Agent identity and registries** (orthogonal, useful to delimit scope): AI Identity gap analysis @arxiv:2604.23280 (2026-04-25); ITU-T Focus Group on Trust and Identity for Humans and Agentic AI (announced 2026-07-09); Cloud Security Alliance draft Agent Registry Specification v1 (2026-03-27).
- **Out-of-window near-duplicates worth knowing** (all predate v1, marked clearly): Translucent Computing "Agentic AI Canvas" (March 2025, consultancy canvas, near-identical name); Abundly "Agent Design Canvas" (October 2025, workshop canvas, content-wise the closest practitioner canvas); MongoDB "Canvas Framework" (October 2025, POC and production canvases); 4D-ARE requirements methodology for LLM agents (@arxiv:2601.04556, January 2026); OASF, the Open Agentic Schema Framework (AGNTCY coalition, ongoing). None has FAIR export or a benefit contract.
- **Machine Learning Canvas status**: unchanged at v1.2, hosted at ownml.co, no agent or generative-AI adaptation found. Strategyzer has released no AI adaptation of the Business Model Canvas. The ML Canvas contrast in beat 3 remains accurate.

## Intro: agentic AI across science

- **Accelerating scientific discovery with Co-Scientist** (Gottweis et al., Google DeepMind, Nature 655:487-496, online 2026-05-19). Multi-agent hypothesis generation validated in three domains, including AML drug repurposing and liver-fibrosis targets in organoids. @doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10644-y
- **Robin: a multi-agent system for automating scientific discovery** (Ghareeb et al., FutureHouse, Nature, online 2026-05-19). Proposed ripasudil for dry AMD; held up in cell lines and primary human tissue. @doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10652-y. Companion News and Views by Elemento: @doi:10.1038/s41586-026-01873-2
- **Biomni: autonomous biomedical research agent** (Huang et al., Stanford, Science, 2026-07-09). 400+ benchmarked tasks across 25 biomedical domains, expert-level accuracy at lower time cost; prototype reportedly in use by 10,000+ labs. @doi:10.1126/science.adz4351
- **The AI Scientist** (Lu et al., Sakana AI, Nature 651:914-919, 2026-03-25). Full ML research cycle automated; an AI-written paper passed workshop peer review. Documents its own limitations (naive ideas, hallucinated citations). @doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10265-5
- **Nature editorial: "AI scientists are changing research; institutions, funders and publishers must respond"** (2026-03-25). Warns of accountability, reproducibility, and disclosure gaps and "automated, large-scale P hacking"; calls for guard rails before automation outpaces norms. High-prominence support for our motivation. @doi:10.1038/d41586-026-00934-w
- **AI agents in clinical practice: an evidence map** (Xu et al., npj Digital Medicine, 2026-07-13). Deployments concentrated in back-office workflows; proactive governance and auditability named prerequisites. Motivation for the clinical strand of the evidence base. @doi:10.1038/s41746-026-02960-4
- **CellVoyager** (Alber et al., Nature Methods, 2026-03-17). Autonomous scRNA-seq analysis agent, peer-reviewed, in our core domain. @doi:10.1038/s41592-026-03029-6
- **OriGene** (bioRxiv v2, 2026-02-25, preprint). Multi-agent target discovery over 600+ MCP tools, organoid-validated nominations; thematically close to the Open Targets case. @doi:10.1101/2025.06.03.657658

## Expectation-realisation gap (beat 2, discussion)

A note on optics: the current gap citation set leans on our own companion paper (@doi:10.48550/arXiv.2602.20292). The items below give independent in-window support.

- **METR experiment update** (2026-02-24, verified). See action item 3. Must accompany @arxiv:2507.09089.
- **The efficiency-gain illusion** (Yu et al., preprint, 2026-05-21). Three preregistered studies, N=2,691: people underestimate how much they use AI and overestimate its time savings, choosing AI even when it saves nothing. The psychological mechanism behind the gap. @arxiv:2605.22687
- **OSWorld 2.0** (preprint, June/July 2026). 108 long-horizon computer-use tasks (median 1.6 hours of human work): best agent completes 20.6% fully, versus ~66% on the short-horizon original. Benchmark numbers overstate realised capability on realistic work by roughly a factor of three. @arxiv:2606.29537
- **Remote Labor Index update** (Center for AI Safety and Scale AI, 2026-07-01). Automation rate on real freelance work rose 2.5% to 15.8% in eight months, yet ~84% of projects still fail client-acceptable quality; their automated judge scored triple what human professionals rated. Supports the gap and shows expectations need dating. @url:https://safe.ai/blog/significant-increase-in-digital-labor-automation
- **General AgentBench** (CMU et al., preprint, 2026-02-22). Substantial degradation when agents leave single-domain benchmarks for unified multi-domain settings; test-time scaling does not recover it. @arxiv:2602.18998
- **Stanford AI Index 2026** (2026-04-13). Citable institutional statement: 88% of organizations use AI somewhere, but agent deployment is single-digit across nearly all business functions while capabilities surge.
- **Gartner 2026 Hype Cycle for Agentic AI** (June 2026). Agentic AI at the Peak of Inflated Expectations; 17% deployed versus 60%+ intending within two years. Primary document paywalled; figures verified via secondary coverage.
- **Why Johnny Can't Use Agents** (Shome, Krishnan, Das, v2 2026-05-03 for a 2026 ACM venue; v1 is pre-window, cite only the venue version). 31 users on Operator and Manus: marketing-shaped expectations do not survive contact. @arxiv:2509.14528
- **Benchmarking LLM agents for single-cell omics analysis** (Liu et al., Genome Biology 27:123, 2026-02-25). Result consistency with ground truth rarely exceeded 0.4 across three frameworks and eight LLMs on 50 real tasks. The gap quantified in our own primary domain. @doi:10.1186/s13059-026-03998-z
- **AI Research Agents Narrow Scientific Exploration** (Tang and Yang, preprint, 2026-05-27). 219,655 agent-generated research ideas: more concentrated, closer to source literature, lower-impact regions than human papers. @arxiv:2605.27905
- **Autonomous Research Agents: survey and the verification gap** (Ding et al., preprint, August 2026). Of 35 AI-scientist systems, 38% ship reproducibility artifacts, 38% report novelty verification; no closed-loop system shows an externally validated in-loop oracle. Pairs well with our documentation argument. @arxiv:2608.05179
- **Agentic AI Scientists Are Not Built For Autonomous Scientific Discovery** (Bisht et al., position preprint, 2026-05-09). Four structural obstacles, argued unfixable by scaling. Counterweight to the Nature flagship papers. @arxiv:2605.08956
- **Honest complication:** a randomized field experiment on 31,000+ preprints found LLM feedback raised revision rates by 12.55% relative, with benefits concentrated among early-career and non-English-dominant researchers (@arxiv:2605.24180, 2026-05-22). Well-scoped, single-purpose deployments with prospectively defined outcomes do deliver, which argues for the AAC's quantified, dated expectation records rather than against them.
- **Checked, no in-window update exists:** MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide" (July 2025 figure still circulating), S&P Global 42% abandonment (March 2025), MAST multi-agent failure taxonomy (October 2025), TheAgentCompany (NeurIPS 2025), both NEJM AI ambient-scribe RCTs (November 2025, one of them is the cited @pmc:PMC12768499).

## Simplified canvas, facilitation gap, elicitation (beats 6, 7, discussion lead, 8, 8.1)

- **ReqElicitGym** (Jin et al., preprint, 2026-02-20). Benchmark of LLMs as elicitation interviewers over 101 scenarios: they elicit less than half of implicit requirements and ask effective questions late. Quantitative support for the facilitation gap. @arxiv:2602.18306
- **OntoAgent** (Jin et al., RE 2026). Encodes interviewer experience as an ontology; +33% implicit-requirement elicitation over plain LLM chat. Partial counter-evidence: the interviewing-skill half of facilitation is being automated. Does not touch anchoring. @arxiv:2605.05828
- **Collaborative and AI-supported requirements elicitation** (Salgado Neto et al., preprint, 2026-06-23). Human collaboration plus AI synthesis beat both plain human collaboration and direct LLM generation. Supports facilitation remaining necessary; also suggests our own facilitated sessions could be AI-augmented. @arxiv:2606.24060
- **The Analytics Translation Problem** (Trabelsi et al., preprint, 2026-04-20). Review of 18 methodologies: none gives systematic guidance for translating a stated business goal into task, data, and metric choices. Positions the canvas as filling a documented methodological gap; supports the operational-detail finding. @arxiv:2604.18770
- **Funding AI for Good: A Call for Meaningful Engagement** (Lin et al., CHI 2026 proceedings, April 2026; cite the proceedings, the preprint is pre-window). 35 funding agendas positioned AI as the solution before problem specifics were established. Institutional evidence for solution-first anchoring. @doi:10.1145/3772318.3790374
- **Generative AI constrains divergent thinking in design** (Lin et al., Frontiers in Psychology, 2026-06-30). Novice designers exposed to AI suggestions at the start of ideation produced less original, less varied concepts and adapted their ideas to the AI's. The anchoring mechanism, measured. @doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1839565
- **Evaluation Cards** (Ghosh et al., Hugging Face-led consortium of 48 authors, preprint, 2026-06-08). From 52 papers, 12 interviews, and 5,816 monitored models: full-depth documentation items block work; their remedy is compact tiered signals in audience-specific reader modes. An independent 2026 effort converging on the simplified-entry design; the nearest in-window evidence for the detail-vs-completion tradeoff. @arxiv:2606.09809
- **LLM facilitation shifts outcomes invisibly** (Parisi et al., FAccT 2026, N=879 with real stakes). LLM facilitators did not improve consensus but shifted group allocations by up to 5.5 points while participants believed procedure had improved. The risk of substituting a tool for a deliberately restrained facilitator. @doi:10.1145/3805689.3812253
- **Human vs LLM facilitator tendencies** (Tsirmpas, Korre, Pavlopoulos, preprint, May 2026). Human expert facilitators prefer not to intervene; LLMs over-intervene. Withholding, the exact behavior of our solution-agnostic first phase, is what LLM facilitators fail to reproduce. @arxiv:2607.28643
- **AInterviewer** (Gardhus et al., preprint, May 2026). Open-source platform for AI-led qualitative interviews with controlled question ordering. In-principle counter-evidence to "cannot be simulated": controlled sequencing is automatable. The participant still knowingly enters an AI interview, and no validation against human interviewers is reported. @arxiv:2606.20588
- **Key contract issues in agentic AI deals** (Mayer Brown LLP, 2026-06-16). Enterprise contracts for agentic AI now enumerate liability for autonomous actions, layered performance measurement, dependency ownership, and exit provisions. The legal market independently concluding that agentic handovers need a written record of mutual obligations; supports beat 8.1. @url:https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/key-contract-issues-in-agentic-ai-implementation-and-integration-deals
- Boundary case: **Agent Contracts** (Ye et al., COINE at AAMAS 2026) formalizes agent contracts with input/output specs; v1 is 2026-01-13, just pre-window (v3 2026-03-25). Usable if the window is read loosely. @arxiv:2601.08815

## FAIR, RO-Crate, Croissant, MCP, AGENTS.md (Methods, beats 8.2, 8.4)

- **AGENTS.md efficiency study** (Lulla et al., ICSE JAWs workshop 2026, April 2026; preprint is late January). First controlled study: 10 repos, 124 PRs, with/without AGENTS.md; 28.64% lower median runtime, 16.58% fewer output tokens, comparable completion. First quantitative support for the AGENTS.md export. @arxiv:2601.20404
- **AGENTS.md institutional adoption** (Agentic AI Foundation, Linux Foundation). 60,000+ repositories, 20+ tools; AAIF grew past 170 member organizations by April 2026 with AWS, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI as platinum members. The format is an institutionally governed standard now.
- **GitHub Spec Kit** (releases through v0.16.3, 2026-08-13; ~111k stars, 30+ agent integrations). Specify-plan-tasks-implement workflow with the spec as the agent's grounding document.
- **Amazon Kiro GA, Amazon Q Developer retired in its favor** (2026-05-07; Q Developer end-of-support announced 2026-04-30). A major vendor replaced its flagship assistant with a spec-first product. Strong support for design contracts preceding code.
- **SpecBench** (preprint, 2026-05-28). Agents critique design proposals from real Kubernetes/React RFCs; best agent 44.4% accuracy. Specification-level reasoning is the frontier; human-authored design documents remain necessary input. @arxiv:2605.30314
- **CodeSpec** (preprint, 2026-07-29). Architecture and behavior specs compiled into executable form measurably steer long-horizon agent development. @arxiv:2607.26777
- **Specification security benchmark** (preprint, 2026-05-29). Enriching the spec with security knowledge cut security failures from 50 to 36; spec content directly determines generated-code quality. @arxiv:2606.00167
- **ADR context study** (Gupta et al., preprint, 2026-04-04). Feeding 3-5 recent architecture decision records as context beats larger models without them; captured design decisions are demonstrably useful LLM context. Core support for beat 8.4 as "planning and context data". @arxiv:2604.03826
- **RO-Crate 1.3** (2026-06-23). Canonical Bioschemas URIs for workflow terms, Schema.org v30.0. Migration from 1.2 is conformsTo plus @context only. Check whether our workflow terms should adopt the canonical URIs; citing the trivial migration shows the format choice is stable. @doi:10.5281/zenodo.20720080
- **Croissant 1.1** (MLCommons, 2026-02-12). PROV-O provenance, DUO and ODRL governance terms, explicitly agent-ready; 700,000+ datasets. Strengthens the Croissant precedent in beat 8.2: an institution-backed format independently converged on our vocabularies and now targets the agentic use case.
- **NIH structured DMSP form** (required from 2026-05-25; DMPTool template 2026-04-24). A major funder moving planning documents from prose to structured, machine-checkable form; librarians' in-window criticism (checkboxes lose design reasoning) is an argument for richer prospective instruments like the canvas.
- **MCP specification 2026-07-28** (Agentic AI Foundation). Stateless redesign, discovery RPC, extensions framework, 12-month deprecation policy. Server metadata becomes more load-bearing.
- **MCP registry status**: 3,012 unique servers as of 2026-03-04, server.json metadata standard, designed as a meta-registry; still officially in preview as of August 2026. Third-party analysis (NimbleBrain, March 2026) finds self-reported metadata often incomplete, which supports the case for verifiable design-time metadata.
- **The metadata ecosystem and AI** (Greenberg et al., AI Magazine, 2026-05-20), part of a special issue on FAIR and AI readiness (intro @doi:10.1002/aaai.70063). Frames machine-actionable metadata as computational infrastructure for trustworthy AI. @doi:10.1002/aaai.70060
- **Do Data Agents Need Semantic Metadata?** (Chen, Alrashed, Halevy, Noy, preprint, 2026-05-27). Agent over semantically annotated datasets achieved 65.7% higher precision than an open-web agent. Empirical support that FAIR metadata pays off specifically for agents. @arxiv:2605.28787
- FAIR4ML: no new version in the window; existing citations need no update.

## Preregistration (beat 8.5)

- **Sequential Evaluation with Model Preregistration** (Kjell et al., Clinical Psychological Science, 2026-05-04). Lock and preregister a model, then test prospectively on a future sample; the preregistered predictions held. A 2026 precedent for commit-then-compare in AI evaluation. @doi:10.1177/21677026261439026
- **Preregistering for the next LLM** (Thomas, Gligoric, Shah, preprint, 2026-06-26). Commit the analysis before the eligible model exists; ~73% of p-hacks failed to transfer. The AI community converging on commitment-before-outcome protocols. @arxiv:2606.27687
